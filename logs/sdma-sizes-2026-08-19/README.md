Where working SDMA helps, and where it hurts. Copies completed in six seconds,
arms alternated per size.

| transfer size | SDMA on | SDMA off | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 KiB | 423456 | 435712 | 0.97 |
| 16384 | 401856 | 395072 | 1.01 |
| 16385 | 397824 | 351744 | 1.13 |
| 64 KiB | 283744 | 268064 | 1.05 |
| 256 KiB | 142080 | 128992 | 1.10 |
| 1 MiB | 39328 | 37920 | 1.03 |
| 16 MiB | 10912 | 41312 | 0.26 |

Three regimes, and the boundary lands exactly where the tracing said it would.

At and below 16384 bytes the two arms are indistinguishable, because ROCclr
stages those through a blit compute kernel and never involves SDMA whatever the
firmware says. That was predicted before measuring and it holds.

Between 16385 bytes and about 1 MiB, SDMA is faster, by 13 percent immediately
above the threshold and 10 percent at 256 KiB. This is the band GabriWar
expected a benefit in, and it is there.

At 16 MiB SDMA is four times slower, 10912 copies against 41312. That is
consistent with the absolute figures measured separately: SDMA sustains about
30 GB/s at that size where the blit path reaches about 110. For bulk transfer
the workaround was faster than the thing it was working around, and the gap
widens with size, the blit path reaching roughly 150 GB/s from 512 MiB upward
where SDMA stays at 47.8.

The blit figure previously read "124 to 152", which was never captured and does
not describe this size. A ten repeat sweep with `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA=0` on 25 August
gives 110.30 GB/s at 16 MiB, and 150.89, 151.05 and 149.59 at 512 MiB, 1 GiB and
2 GiB; 124 to 152 is closer to that large transfer band than to 16 MiB. The
four times ratio the table reports is unaffected, since 110 against SDMA's 30 is
the same factor the copy counts show. Sweep in
[`blit-recheck-2026-08-25/`](blit-recheck-2026-08-25/).

So "SDMA now works" and "SDMA should be enabled" are different questions. It
helps a band of medium transfers and costs heavily on large ones, and llama.cpp
inference sits far enough from both that enabling it moves decode by 0.1 percent
(see `../sdma-firmware-2026-08-19/`). An earlier version of that directory's note
said the fix "does not make anything faster here", which was too broad: it does
not help *that* workload, but the band above the threshold is a real gain.
