# Is the KQV corruption periodic across processes? No, 2026-08-21

Generated by `scripts/kqv_periodicity.sh`.

## Why

The context ladder run earlier the same night put the catastrophic result at the
third invocation of every rung, positions 3, 6 and 9 of nine runs. It was noted that
landing on the last of each group three times running would be about a 0.3
percent coincidence if the runs failed independently, and flagged it as possibly
something accumulating across processes. Position and context were confounded
there, since the ladder changed context every three runs, so twelve consecutive
identical invocations at one context were needed to separate them.

## Result: it was a coincidence

Twelve consecutive runs, unpatched build, qwen2.5-1.5B Q4_K_M at context 2048,
chunks 2, `-fa off`. Correct is 10.0103.

    1  343.5732     7  115.8450
    2  1057.5494    8  91.3845
    3  85.0876      9  10.0103
    4  1491.3304   10  10.0103
    5  10.0103     11  1994.7835
    6  115.8450    12  116.4559

Wrong at positions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 11 and 12. There is no period. The
ladder pattern was chance, and the 0.3 percent figure attached to it is a
reminder of why a probability computed for a pattern noticed after looking at
the data is worth very little. The pattern was real in the sense that it
happened; it just did not mean anything.

## Two things this does show

**The failure rate is much higher than the ladder suggested.** Three of twelve
runs are correct here, against two of three at the same context in the ladder.
Pooling both gives five correct in fifteen at context 2048, so the defect
strikes about two runs in three rather than one in three. Three samples per
condition was not enough to say anything about rate, and the ladder write-up
should be read with that in mind.

**The wrongness is discrete, not continuous.** Nine distinct values across
twelve runs, but 10.0103 appears three times and 115.8450 twice, both exactly
repeated. Accumulated rounding error would not reproduce a wrong answer to four
decimal places. Something is selecting between a small number of behaviours, and
the correct answer is one of them. A dispatch trace comparing a correct run with
a 115.8450 run would say whether a different GEMM kernel is chosen, which is the
obvious next step.

It was done, later the same day, and the answer is no: eight traced runs all issue
448 calls with 8 distinct signatures, and a correct run's signatures are identical
to a wrong run's once two scalars are masked, so rocBLAS is asked for the same
thing every time and does not always return the same answer
([`../kqv-dispatch-2026-08-21/`](../kqv-dispatch-2026-08-21/)). That put the
variation below the library interface, which is where the later work looked.
