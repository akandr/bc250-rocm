Counterbalanced repeats of two comparisons that had been run as blocks, and the
reason the blocked versions are not trustworthy.

**Part A, graph capture, ABBA order, 8B at depth 16128.** Six runs per arm,
alternated within each round so linear drift cancels.

| round | capture off | capture on | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 17.70 | 15.78 | +1.92 |
| 2 | 19.78 | 17.89 | +1.89 |
| 3 | 19.69 | 17.12 | +2.57 |

Mean difference +2.13 t/s with sd 0.38, the same sign in three rounds of three,
so disabling graph capture is about 13 percent **faster** at this depth.

The blocked version of the same comparison, ten runs of one arm followed by ten
of the other, reported the opposite: disabling capture 15 percent slower. Both
cannot be right, and the blocked design is the one that cannot separate the flag
from anything drifting over the ninety minutes it took. The earlier figure is
withdrawn and the direction with it.

**Part B, three models at depth 8192, model order rotated each round.** Six runs
each, graph capture on throughout so the flag is not in play.

Variability does not rise with bandwidth utilisation: the middle model is again
the most variable. The blocked version of this comparison had produced a
monotone ordering (2.8, 5.1, 6.6 percent) that looked like support for the
bandwidth explanation, and it does not survive rotating the order. The
explanation stays untested and unsupported.

Clock and temperature are sampled per run. The 1.5B holds 1500 MHz while the two
larger models sit at 1000 MHz for the sampled instant, which is a difference
between models rather than between runs of one model.
